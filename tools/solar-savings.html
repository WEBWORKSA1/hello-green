    How this calculator works

    We divide your bill by your price per kWh to estimate yearly use, then size a system to cover the share you chose. Output assumes 80% of the panels’ rated power reaches your home after inverter, wiring, heat and dirt losses, and that output falls 0.5% a year as panels age.

    Savings assume you use about 40% of your solar power as it is made. The rest goes to the grid and is credited at the export rate you set. Fixed monthly charges on your bill do not change with solar, so a bill with a large connection fee will overstate usage slightly. The payback period is when cumulative savings pass the cost after rebates.

    What it leaves out: financing interest, a possible inverter replacement around year 12–15, roof repairs, time-of-use rates and battery storage. Those can move payback by a few years either way, which is why written quotes matter.

    Reading your result

    • Under 8 years: a strong case in most places. Get quotes.
    • 8–12 years: solid if you plan to stay in the home and your roof has 15+ years of life left.
    • Over 12 years: look at efficiency first, check community solar, or wait for a better price per watt.
