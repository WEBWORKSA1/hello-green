      Method and sources

      Home energy uses your country’s average grid factor (kilograms of CO₂ per kWh) and standard combustion factors: about 0.18 kg CO₂ per kWh of natural gas, 2.68 kg per litre of heating oil and 2.31 kg per litre of gasoline. Household energy is divided by the number of people in the home; travel, food and shopping are counted per person.

      Flights use about 95 kg CO₂ per passenger flight-hour, which leaves out the extra warming from contrails and other high-altitude effects, so treat flight numbers as a floor. Diet figures range from about 1.5 tonnes a year for a vegan diet to 3.3 tonnes for meat at most meals, in line with widely used lifecycle studies. Shopping is a broad average for clothes, electronics and household goods.

      Country comparisons use approximate per-person CO₂ emissions from fossil fuels and industry (Our World in Data, latest year). Those national figures count emissions where they are produced, while this calculator counts what you consume, so the two are close cousins rather than identical twins. The 2-tonne line is a commonly cited per-person level consistent with the Paris Agreement goals by around 2050.
