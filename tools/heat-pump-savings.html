    How the estimate works

    Your yearly spend divided by your fuel price gives the amount of fuel you buy. Multiplying by its energy content and your system’s efficiency gives the useful heat your home needs. A heat pump with a seasonal coefficient of performance (COP) of 3 delivers 3 kWh of heat for every 1 kWh of electricity, so its electricity use is that heat divided by the COP.

    Energy content used: 29.3 kWh per therm and 10.55 kWh per cubic metre of natural gas, 10.6 kWh per litre of heating oil, 7.0 kWh per litre of propane. Carbon factors: 0.184 kg CO₂ per kWh of gas, 0.25 for oil, 0.215 for propane, plus your country’s average grid factor for electricity.

    When a heat pump costs more to run, it is usually because gas is cheap relative to electricity where you live. Time-of-use tariffs, a higher-COP model, better insulation and solar panels can all flip the result. A heat pump also replaces your air conditioner, so if your AC is near the end of its life, count the avoided AC cost too.
