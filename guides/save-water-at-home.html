Save water at home: fixes ranked by impact

Leaks, showers and toilets use most household water. Fix them in the right order and you cut both your water bill and the energy used to heat water.

Hello.Green editorial team. Updated 19 November 2026. 3 minute read.

Quick facts

  • The US EPA estimates household leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons a year in the average home
  • A tap dripping once a second wastes thousands of litres a year
  • Saving hot water also saves energy
  • WaterSense-labelled fixtures use at least 20% less water than standard models

Saving water at home has a double payoff. You pay less to the water utility, and every litre of hot water you do not use is energy you do not pay to heat. Water heating is one of the largest energy uses in most homes, so the shower is where water and energy savings meet.

Here are the fixes in order of how much they usually save for the effort.

1. Find and fix leaks

The US EPA estimates that household leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons (about 38,000 litres) a year in the average home, and that about one home in ten has leaks wasting 90 gallons or more every day.

Check the meter. Note your water meter reading, use no water for two hours, and read it again. If it moved, you have a leak.

Test toilets. Put a few drops of food colouring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Colour in the bowl means a leaking flapper, usually a cheap DIY replacement.

Fix dripping taps. A worn washer or cartridge is often the cause. A tap dripping once a second wastes thousands of litres a year.

Check outdoors. Hose connections, irrigation lines and sprinkler heads leak out of sight.

2. Shorten and slow your showers

A standard showerhead in the US flows at up to 2.5 gallons (9.5 litres) per minute. WaterSense-labelled showerheads use no more than 2.0 gallons (7.6 litres) per minute and still have to meet performance standards. Many low-flow heads go lower while still feeling strong.

Cutting a 10-minute shower to 5 minutes halves the water and the energy used to heat it. A shower timer or a playlist with a few songs makes it easy.

3. Upgrade old toilets

Toilets are typically the largest indoor water user. Older models can use 13 litres (3.5 gallons) or more per flush; modern high-efficiency toilets use 4.8 litres (1.28 gallons) or less, and dual-flush models offer a smaller flush for liquids. If you are not replacing the toilet yet, check that the fill valve is set correctly and the flapper seals.

4. Run full loads

Dishwashers and washing machines use about the same water whether full or half-empty. Run them full. Modern dishwashers usually use less water than washing the same dishes by hand under a running tap. Skip the pre-rinse: scrape plates instead. Wash clothes in cold water to save the energy that heats it.

5. Fit tap aerators

Kitchen and bathroom aerators mix air into the flow, cutting water use while keeping pressure feeling normal. They cost very little and screw on in a minute.

6. Water the garden wisely

Outdoor watering can be a large share of summer water use.

  • Water early in the morning, when less evaporates.
  • Water deeply and less often to encourage deep roots.
  • Use mulch around plants to hold moisture.
  • Choose native and drought-tolerant plants.
  • Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses instead of sprinklers for beds.
  • Collect rainwater in a barrel where local rules allow it.
  • Set mowers higher; longer grass shades its own roots.

7. Insulate hot water pipes and lower the temperature

Insulating hot water pipes means hot water arrives faster, so you run the tap less while waiting. Setting the water heater to about 49°C (120°F) saves energy and reduces scald risk. Keep it higher if anyone in the home has a weakened immune system, and follow local guidance on legionella.

What it adds up to

A household that fixes leaks, installs a low-flow showerhead and aerators, and shortens showers can often cut water use by a fifth or more, with a matching cut in water-heating energy. Most of these fixes cost little and pay back within months.

For energy savings that go with them, try the home energy quick wins calculator, and for a full walkthrough of your home, the 30-minute DIY audit.

The bottom line

Fix leaks first, then the shower, then the toilet. Run full loads and water the garden in the morning. It is the cheapest set of green upgrades in the house, and every hot litre you save is energy you do not pay for.
