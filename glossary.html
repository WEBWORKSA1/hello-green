Green home glossary

The words installers, utilities and labels use, explained in a sentence or two.

  • AFUEAnnual Fuel Utilization Efficiency: the share of a furnace or boiler’s fuel that becomes useful heat. A 95% AFUE furnace wastes 5%.
  • Air sealingClosing gaps and cracks in a home’s shell so heated or cooled air cannot leak out. Usually the cheapest, highest-payback efficiency upgrade.
  • Air-source heat pumpA heat pump that moves heat between outdoor air and your home. Works for heating and cooling, including in cold climates with modern models.
  • BaseloadThe electricity your home uses all the time, day and night, from fridges, routers and devices on standby.
  • Battery storageA home battery that stores electricity from solar or the grid for later use or backup during outages.
  • BiodegradableAble to be broken down by microorganisms. Without a timeframe and conditions, the label means little.
  • Blower-door testA professional test that depressurises a home with a fan to measure how leaky it is and find air leaks.
  • BTUBritish thermal unit, a unit of heat energy. 1 kWh is about 3,412 BTU.
  • Carbon footprintThe total greenhouse gas emissions caused by a person, household, product or organisation, usually in tonnes of CO₂e per year.
  • Carbon offsetA credit representing a tonne of emissions reduced or removed elsewhere. Quality varies widely, so reducing your own emissions comes first.
  • CO₂eCarbon dioxide equivalent: a way to express different greenhouse gases as the amount of CO₂ that would cause the same warming.
  • Community solarA shared solar farm that households subscribe to for a bill credit, without panels on their own roof.
  • CompostDecomposed organic matter used to improve soil. Composting food scraps keeps them out of landfills, where they would release methane.
  • COPCoefficient of performance: units of heat a heat pump delivers per unit of electricity. A COP of 3 means 3 kWh of heat for 1 kWh of electricity.
  • Demand chargeA charge based on your highest burst of power use in a billing period, measured in kW.
  • Derate factorThe share of a solar system’s rated output that actually reaches your home after losses from heat, wiring, inverters and dirt.
  • Ductless mini-splitA heat pump with indoor wall or ceiling units connected to an outdoor unit, needing no ducts.
  • Embodied carbonEmissions from making, transporting and installing a product or building, as opposed to running it.
  • EnerGuideCanada’s official rating of home energy performance, used by federal and provincial retrofit programs.
  • Energy auditAn assessment of where a home wastes energy, done yourself or by a professional with diagnostic tools.
  • ENERGY STARA US EPA program certifying products and homes that meet energy-efficiency specifications.
  • EVElectric vehicle, powered by a battery charged from the grid rather than by burning fuel.
  • Export tariffThe rate your utility or supplier pays for solar power you send to the grid. Also called a feed-in tariff or export credit.
  • Feed-in tariffA payment per kWh for electricity a home generates and sends to the grid.
  • Geothermal heat pumpA ground-source heat pump that moves heat between your home and the ground through buried pipes.
  • GreenwashingMarketing that makes a product or company seem more environmentally friendly than it is.
  • Grid emission factorThe average kilograms of CO₂ released per kWh of electricity on a given grid. Clean grids are near zero; coal-heavy grids are high.
  • Heat pump water heaterA water heater that uses heat pump technology, typically using a fraction of the electricity of a standard electric tank.
  • HERS IndexA US home energy rating score; lower numbers mean a more efficient home.
  • Induction cooktopAn electric cooktop that heats pans directly with a magnetic field. Fast, efficient and free of combustion fumes.
  • InverterThe device that converts solar panels’ direct current (DC) into alternating current (AC) for your home.
  • kWKilowatt: a unit of power, the rate of energy use or production at a moment.
  • kWhKilowatt-hour: a unit of energy. Running a 1,000-watt appliance for an hour uses 1 kWh.
  • Level 2 chargerA 240-volt home EV charger that adds roughly 30–50 km of range per hour.
  • Low-E glassWindow glass with a thin coating that reflects heat, keeping homes warmer in winter and cooler in summer.
  • MCSMicrogeneration Certification Scheme: the UK standard installers must meet for heat pumps and solar to qualify for grants.
  • MethaneA greenhouse gas far more potent than CO₂ over the short term, released by landfills, livestock and gas leaks.
  • Net meteringA billing arrangement where solar power you export offsets power you buy, usually at the full retail rate.
  • Net zeroA state where greenhouse gas emissions released are balanced by those removed from the atmosphere.
  • Off-peakHours when electricity demand and often prices are lower, usually overnight.
  • Payback periodHow long it takes for an upgrade’s savings to equal its upfront cost.
  • Peak sun hoursThe equivalent number of hours per day of full-strength sunlight a location receives. Used to estimate solar output.
  • Phantom loadElectricity drawn by devices that are switched off or on standby. Also called standby or vampire power.
  • PPAPower purchase agreement: a company installs and owns solar on your roof and sells you the power at an agreed rate.
  • R-valueA measure of insulation’s resistance to heat flow. Higher is better.
  • RebateMoney returned after buying or installing a qualifying product, from a government, utility or manufacturer.
  • Renewable energyEnergy from sources that are naturally replenished, such as sun, wind, water and geothermal heat.
  • SEER2Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio: a US rating of air conditioner and heat pump cooling efficiency.
  • Smart thermostatA thermostat that learns schedules or adjusts automatically to cut heating and cooling when you are out or asleep.
  • Standby powerSee phantom load.
  • Time-of-use rateAn electricity tariff with different prices at different times of day.
  • U-valueA measure of how much heat passes through a material or window. Lower is better. The inverse of R-value.
  • UpcyclingTurning waste or unwanted items into something of higher value or use.
  • VermicompostingComposting with worms, which turn food scraps into nutrient-rich castings. Works indoors.
  • WeatherstrippingStrips of material that seal gaps around doors and windows.
  • Zero wasteAn approach that aims to send nothing to landfill by refusing, reducing, reusing, recycling and composting.
