      Where these numbers come from

      LEDs use about 75–85% less electricity than the incandescent and halogen bulbs they replace, so a 60 W bulb becomes roughly 9 W. Savings scale with hours of use, which is why the kitchen and living room come first.

      Standby power runs 24 hours a day. Ten devices at 4 W each is 40 W of constant draw, or about 350 kWh a year. Smart power strips and plugs cut it without changing your routine.

      Thermostat setbacks: the US Department of Energy estimates you can save up to 10% a year on heating and cooling by turning the thermostat back 7–10°F (about 4–5.5°C) for 8 hours a day. We use roughly 2% per °C, capped at 10%.

      Water heater: each 5–6°C (10°F) reduction cuts water heating energy by roughly 3–5%. We use 0.7% per °C. Do not go below 49°C (120°F), and keep stored water at 60°C if anyone in the home is immune-compromised, because of legionella risk.

      Laundry: most of a washing machine’s energy goes into heating water, so modern detergents and cold cycles save most of it.
